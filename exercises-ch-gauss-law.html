    8.

    Use Gauss’ Law with an appropriate choice of Gaussian surface to derive the magnitude of the electric field at a distance \(r\) from the center of a spherical distribution of charge. The sphere is solid, with radius = \(a\) and charge \(Q\text{,}\) and the charges are uniformly distributed throughout the sphere’s volume. Do the calculation only for the case where \(r \lt a\) (i.e., we want the electric field inside of the sphere). You will get the following answer: \(E = \frac{Qr}{4\pi {\varepsilon }_{0}a^{3}}\text{.}\) (Hint: partway through the problem, in order to get a general formula in symbols for the charge inside of the distance \(r\text{,}\) you will need a general formula in symbols for the volume inside of the distance \(r\text{.}\))

    15.

    An infinite cylinder of radius \(R\) has a linear charge density \(\lambda\text{.}\) The volume charge density \(\left( \mathrm{\frac{C}{m^{3}}} \right)\) within the cylinder \((r \lt R)\) is \(\rho (r) = rR\rho_{0} \text{,}\) where \(\rho_{0}\) is a constant to be determined.
    1. The charge within a small volume \(dV\) is \(dq = \rho dV\text{.}\) The integral of \(\rho dV\) over a cylinder of length \(L\) is the total charge \(Q = \lambda L\) within the cylinder. Use this fact to to determine the constant \(\rho_{0}\) in terms of \(\lambda\) and \(R\text{.}\) Hint: Let \(dV\) be a cylindrical shell of length \(L\text{,}\) radius \(r\text{,}\) and thickness \(dr\text{.}\) What is the volume of such a shell? Express your answer in terms of the variables \(λ\text{,}\) \(R\text{,}\) and appropriate constants.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Use Gauss’s law to find an expression for the electric field \(\vec{E}\) inside the cylinder, \(r \lt R\text{.}\) Give your answer as a multiple of \(\sfrac{\lambda}{\varepsilon_{0}}\text{.}\) Express your answer in terms of the variables \(r\text{,}\) \(R\text{,}\) and appropriate constants.
