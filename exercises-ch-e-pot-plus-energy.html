    1.

    (Fill in the blanks in the preformatted solution steps below.) An electron is to be accelerated from a speed of \(3.00 \times 10^{6} \: m/s\) to \(8.00 \times 10^{6} \: m/s\text{.}\) Through what potential difference \(\Delta V\) must the electron pass to accomplish this?
    🔗
    Since we are given initial speed and final speed, but no displacements, times, or accelerations, we probably don’t have enough info to use the Equations of \(\fillinmath{XXXXXXX}\) on this problem. Since the source of the electrical effects is not given, we probably don’t have enough info to use a free-body diagram on this problem. Therefore, we probably need to use Conservation of Energy to solve the problem:
    \begin{equation*} E_{i}=E_{f} \end{equation*}
    If the only sources of energy are kinetic and electric potential, then the equation breaks into:
    \begin{equation*} {KE}_{i}+U_{electric,i}={\fillinmath{XX}}_{f}+{\fillinmath{XX}}_{f} \end{equation*}
    We can substitute in the known equations for \(KE\) and \(U_{electric}\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} \frac{1}{2}\fillinmath{XX} \: {{\fillinmath{XX}}_{i}}^{2}+q{\fillinmath{XX}}_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\fillinmath{XX} \: {{\fillinmath{XX}}_{f}}^{2}+q{\fillinmath{XX}}_{f} \end{equation*}
    Then, we can solve for the unknown \(\Delta V\text{.}\) Note \(\Delta V\) is defined as \(V_{f} - V_{i}\text{,}\) so we want to get \(V_{f} - V_{i}\) on one side of the equation.
    \begin{align*} \frac{1}{2}\fillinmath{XXX} - \frac{1}{2}\fillinmath{XXX} \amp = +q\fillinmath{XX} - q \fillinmath{XX}\\ \\ \frac{1}{2}m(\fillinmath{XX} - \fillinmath{XX}) \amp = q(V_{f}-V_{i})\\ \\ \frac{m}{2q}( \fillinmath{XX} - \fillinmath{XX}) \amp = (V_{f}-V_{i}) \end{align*}
    The test particle that is being accelerated by the source of the electrical effects is the electron. So, we need to use the mass of the electron and charge of the electron:
    \begin{equation*} V_{f}-V_{i}=\frac{9.109 \times {10}^{-31} \: kg}{2(- \fillinmath{XXXXX} \: C)} ((\fillinmath{XXXXX})^{2} - (\fillinmath{XXXXX})^{2}) \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \Delta V = +156V \end{equation*}
    Addendum: Once you know \(\Delta V\text{,}\) in some problems you may be able to use that to deduce what the source charges were. But in this problem, we are stopping here.

    3.

    A proton \(\left( charge = +1.602 \times 10^{-19} \: C \right)\) with an initial speed of \(8.30 \times 10^{5} \: \frac{m}{s}\) is reduced to a final speed of \(2.70 \times 10^{5} \: \frac{m}{s}\) by an electric field. Did the proton move into a region of higher (more positive) potential or lower (more negative) potential? What was the potential difference \((V_{f}-V_{i})\) that slowed the proton, including sign? This shows that protons would prefer to roll “down” potential hills.
